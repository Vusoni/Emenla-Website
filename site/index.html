Your words, their words

Your words, their words.

Tell it in your own words. We'll find the clinical name for it.

You write what you feel. Emenla matches it to the term a clinician would recognise, shows you where that term comes from, and keeps your own sentence printed right beside it. A clinician can check the match at a glance. So can you.

Emenla names symptoms in clinical language. It never names a condition.

How it works

Three things it does.

01

A few words is enough.

Type or speak. "Bad cramps, back pain, stayed in bed" is a complete entry. Add severity and a period start if you want to. Skip everything else.

02

See what you logged.

Counts from your own entries. Which days, how often, how bad, and how that lines up with your logged periods. Nothing forecast, nothing scored, no streaks.

03

Walk in with proof.

A one-page summary in clinical terms, with your own words printed beside each one, for you to bring to your appointment. What it is for is you feeling clear about your own body. Whether a clinician reads it is up to them.

Three centuries from the first description to a guideline that says your symptoms are enough.

1690
1690Described

First written down.

Daniel Shroen, a German physician, describes ulcers in and around the uterus that match what we now call endometriosis.

1860
1860Seen

Seen under a microscope.

Karl von Rokitansky in Vienna identifies tissue like the lining of the womb growing where it should not.

1927
1927Named

Given its name.

John Sampson coins the word endometriosis and proposes that backward menstrual flow seeds the tissue. Still the leading theory.

1980
1980Organised
1stpatient-led organisation

Patients organise.

The Endometriosis Association is founded in Milwaukee, the first patient-led organisation, and starts collecting women's own accounts.

2005
2005Guided

The first European guideline.

ESHRE publishes its first guideline on diagnosing and treating endometriosis, updated in 2014 and 2022.

2017
2017Heard

Clinicians are told to listen.

The UK guideline NG73 tells clinicians to suspect endometriosis when a woman reports pelvic pain, period pain that disrupts daily life, or pain during sex.

2026
2026Enough
Enough.symptoms · history · examination

Your symptoms are enough.

ACOG's guideline says a clinical diagnosis from symptoms, history and an examination is enough to begin treatment. Surgery is not required first.

Studies still put the delay between first symptoms and a diagnosis at four to eleven years. Emenla is for the years in between.

Privacy

Everything you write stays on this phone.

There is no account and nothing to sign into. Your entries are not uploaded to a server, not sold, and not shared with anyone.

Kept on this phone

  • Data lives in the app's own private on-device database. There is no server and no user database.
  • Speech recognition runs on the device. No audio leaves the phone and no recording is kept.
  • Photos are copied into the app's private storage. They are never uploaded and never written back to your photo library.
  • No analytics of any kind. No ads, no ad identifiers, no third-party trackers, no crash reporting, no remote configuration, no tracking prompt.
  • No account, nothing to sign into. Emenla does not collect an email address.
  • Notifications are local only. No push service, no device token.
  • The one exception, said plainly. Logging is free forever. The optional summary export is an Apple in-app purchase handled through RevenueCat, which receives an anonymous purchase identity and your purchase status. It never receives your symptoms, notes, photos, cycle dates or your name.
  • Under GDPR, health data is special category data under Article 9. That is exactly why Emenla has no server. There is nothing to breach and nothing to hand over.

Ask a question
What is Emenla?

Emenla is an iPhone app for keeping a record of endometriosis symptoms. You log a day in a few words, typed or spoken. The app counts what you wrote back to you: which days, how often, how bad, and how that lines up with the periods you logged. When you need it, it prints a one-page summary in clinical terms with your own words beside each one, to take to an appointment. Everything stays on the phone.

Is Emenla on the App Store?

Not yet. It is an iPhone app and it is being built now. This page will carry the App Store link the day it is listed.

Does Emenla tell me whether I have endometriosis?

No, and it never will. Emenla names symptoms in clinical language and keeps your words beside each term. Whether those symptoms add up to a condition is a conversation for you and a clinician.

Is it private and secure?

Everything you write lives in the app's own database on your phone. There is no account, no server and no copy anywhere else, so there is nothing for us to read and no symptom data of yours sitting on someone else's machine. Speech recognition runs on the device and no audio is kept. The app has no analytics, no ads and no trackers; this site has no cookies and no third-party fonts. The only thing that ever leaves the phone is an anonymous purchase status, and only if you buy the optional export.

What happens to my entries if I change phones?

You will lose them unless you export first. There is no account and no cloud copy, on purpose. Export before you switch.

Is there a streak, a score, or a daily reminder I will feel bad about?

No. There are no streaks anywhere in Emenla and there never will be. Reminders are optional and never leave your phone.

Does Emenla predict my period or my flares?

No. Emenla does not predict anything. You can log period start dates and the app counts your logged days against them, looking backwards at what you wrote. It never forecasts what is coming.

How is Emenla science-backed exactly?

In the wording, not in a claim about your health. The terms Emenla puts on the page follow the published endometriosis guidelines, ESHRE 2022, NICE NG73 and ACOG 2026, and every figure on this site is printed with the source next to it. What the app itself does is count what you logged; it never predicts. Emenla has not been studied in a clinical trial and no clinician has reviewed it yet, and the science page says so as plainly as it says the rest. Read the science behind Emenla.

Are you affiliated with a brand or a clinic?

No. Emenla is an independent project built in Poland, with no clinic, brand, pharmaceutical or supplement sponsor behind it, and no advertising anywhere in the app or on this site. It recommends no products and sells nothing except the optional summary export. Nothing you write is sold or shared, because it never leaves your phone.

What does it cost?

Logging is free, always, and cannot be put behind a paywall because there is no account or server to lock. The optional doctor-summary export is $34.99 a year or $6.99 a month, one tier, through Apple's in-app purchase. It is not purchasable yet.

Who makes Emenla?

An independent project built in Poland. Not a clinic, not a brand, and not funded by one. The operating entity is named in full on the privacy and terms pages before the app is released.
